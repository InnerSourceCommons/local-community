# InnerSource Commons Portland

Welcome to the Portland, Oregon chapter of [InnerSource Commons](https://innersourcecommons.org/), a local group expanding InnerSource in the community.

InnerSource means applying open source practices, such as open collaboration, shared code, and transparent decision making, to software development within a company. If you practice it, lead it, or are considering starting it at work, this community is for you.

## Charter

### Purpose

InnerSource Commons Portland expands InnerSource use locally by helping practitioners launch, grow, and improve it in their organizations and share firsthand lessons, including failures.

### Who it's for

* People practicing or leading InnerSource: software engineers and managers, platform and developer experience teams, and open source program office (OSPO) staff.
* People starting out who plan to apply InnerSource where they work.
* People in the Portland metro area first, with the wider Pacific Northwest
  welcome.

### What we do

* Hold monthly meetups built around practitioner experience reports and open
  discussion.
* Help newcomers get started by connecting them with experienced members and
  with InnerSource Commons resources such as the
  [patterns](https://patterns.innersourcecommons.org/) and the
  [Learning Path](https://innersourcecommons.org/learn/learning-path/).
* Contribute what we learn back to the global InnerSource Commons as talks,
  case studies, and patterns.

### What we don't do

* No product pitches, sales, or recruiting at events. Vendors and recruiters may attend as practitioners.
* Reveal speakers' or participants' identities or affiliations when sharing meetup insights. Meetups follow the [Chatham House Rule](https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chathamhouse-rule): information may be shared without identifying speakers or other participants, unless a speaker explicitly permits attribution of their own remarks.

### How we run

* Everyone follows the InnerSource Commons
  [Code of Conduct](../CODE-OF-CONDUCT.md).
* We work in the open: plans, materials, and meeting notes live in this
  directory.
* Local organizers make decisions by consensus in the
  [#chapter-portland] Slack channel and record them in `minutes/`.

## Getting involved

You become a member the moment you join us. There is nothing formal to sign up
for. 🎉

1. Join the InnerSource Commons Slack with the
   [invite link](https://innersourcecommons.org/slack).
2. Say hello in [#chapter-portland] and tell us what you're working on.
3. Come to a meetup. We meet monthly, and every event is on the public
   [InnerSource Commons calendar] and announced in [#chapter-portland].

Know someone in Portland working on InnerSource? Invite them. Most of our
members arrive through a colleague.

### Help lead the community

We're looking for local organizers. If you'd like to help, say so
in [#chapter-portland]. 

## Strategy

### Where we are

Our new community meets monthly, giving Portland’s dispersed InnerSource practitioners a place to compare notes with peers facing similar challenges.

### Our approach

Grow through personal invitations and make every meetup worth a busy practitioner's evening. Prioritize a returning, supportive group over large attendance.

### What we'll do

1. **Form a core.** Recruit organizers and initial practitioners by reaching out to locals already doing InnerSource work.
2. **Grow by referral.** Ask each member to invite a colleague working on or interested in starting InnerSource.
3. **Keep a steady, small format.** Meet monthly to share one experience, then discuss in a group small enough for everyone to participate.
4. **Source talks from members.** Prioritize members’ first-hand stories over introductory overviews, and help first-time speakers prepare.
5. **Partner selectively.** Co-host occasional events with overlapping local communities, such as platform engineering, DevOps, and OSPO groups.
6. **Stay connected to the global community.** Share materials upstream, invite visiting InnerSource Commons speakers, and direct members to global events.

### How we'll know it's working

Once a year, the organizers review:

* How many attendees come back to a second and third meetup.
* How many members have presented, written, or organized something.
* How many local organizations are represented, and how many started or expanded InnerSource after joining?
* What we've contributed back to InnerSource Commons.

## What's in this directory

- `materials/`: talks, case studies, and reference material from local events.
- `brand/`: logos and virtual backgrounds for the local community.
- `minutes/`: notes from local meetups and organizer meetings.
